**Scaling Expert**
1. At `10,000` assertions, I would expect single-assertion corrections to truncate rarely, probably `<5%` and often `<1-2%`, because the graph being built here is still fairly sparse: relationship extraction is capped to `[:5]` per search-analysis batch and per deep-read document, and propagation only traverses five link types: [engine.py:2036](/C:/Users/devan/OneDrive/Desktop/Projects/legal-rlm/src/irys/rlm/engine.py#L2036), [engine.py:2445](/C:/Users/devan/OneDrive/Desktop/Projects/legal-rlm/src/irys/rlm/engine.py#L2445), [graph.py:317](/C:/Users/devan/OneDrive/Desktop/Projects/legal-rlm/src/irys/matter/graph.py#L317). The danger zone is batched passes: once you seed roughly `150-250` assertions, truncation becomes plausible; above `500`, it is deterministic. Recommendation: keep `500` as a floor only. Scale budget by frontier size, not raw matter size. For a 10k-assertion matter I would allow batch passes to reach roughly `1.5k-2k` work, capped, instead of hard-stopping at `500`.
2. Under `3` concurrent workers, realistic OCC conflict rate in the BFS hot path should be low single digits of write-attempted nodes, and usually `<1%` of total dequeues, because no-op nodes skip `BEGIN IMMEDIATE`: [belief_revision.py:402](/C:/Users/devan/OneDrive/Desktop/Projects/legal-rlm/src/irys/matter/belief_revision.py#L402). `3` retries per node is probably enough for `3` workers. The bigger failure mode is not retry-cap exhaustion; it is budget erosion from immediate requeues. If same-matter concurrency is expected, I would serialize belief revision per matter rather than increase retries.
3. I would not rotate unannotated searches across all `20` issues. `_initial_searches` is capped at `5`: [engine.py:1305](/C:/Users/devan/OneDrive/Desktop/Projects/legal-rlm/src/irys/rlm/engine.py#L1305). The engine already adds up to `3` predicate leads and `2` SPO leads to the weakest issue: [engine.py:1374](/C:/Users/devan/OneDrive/Desktop/Projects/legal-rlm/src/irys/rlm/engine.py#L1374), [engine.py:1394](/C:/Users/devan/OneDrive/Desktop/Projects/legal-rlm/src/irys/rlm/engine.py#L1394). Later iterations also reweight toward weak issues: [engine.py:1463](/C:/Users/devan/OneDrive/Desktop/Projects/legal-rlm/src/irys/rlm/engine.py#L1463). So the fallback should target the weakest `3`, weighted toward the weakest, for example `W1, W1, W2, W1, W3`. If attribution confidence is low, neutral is safer than wrong-issue assignment.
